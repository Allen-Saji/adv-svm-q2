Block Building on Solana

From the leader's TPU pipeline all the way up to Harmonic's multi-builder marketplace and what Alpenglow + APE do to all of it. Written to be read in one sitting as a standalone reference on Solana block building.

Focus: Harmonic (harmonic.gg) Scope: TPU pipeline to open block building Read time: ~25 min

01The 30-Second Mental Model

The one frame to hold onto before everything else.

On Ethereum, proposers and builders are separate roles. The validator who is up to propose a slot doesn't decide what goes in the block -- they pick the most lucrative pre-built block from a market of builders (MEV-Boost, ePBS). It's an explicit auction with a defined interface.

On Solana, the leader IS the block builder. The validator whose 4-slot window is currently active runs the entire TPU pipeline in-process: receives transactions, schedules them, executes them, hashes them into PoH, and broadcasts. There is no native interface for an outside builder to hand the leader a pre-built block.

Every block-building company on Solana -- Jito, Harmonic, Paladin, BAM, Temporal -- exists to influence the leader's scheduling decisions without breaking that monolithic model. They are overlays, not replacements. The structural fight today is over how much they should influence it, who gets to do the influencing, and whether that should be one operator or many.

★ Anchor insight

Solana block building isn't a market -- it's an integration pattern. The economic and political fight is over what shape that integration takes: single-builder client fork (Jito), multi-builder aggregation (Harmonic), TEE-secured sequencing (BAM), or eventually protocol-level builder roles (APE + Constellation).

Leader schedule is computed once per epoch from the previous epoch's stake state. Every validator independently arrives at the same schedule -- no coordination needed.

Leaders are chosen by stake-weighted pseudorandom selection. 2% of stake → roughly 2% of slots, in 4-slot chunks. Slot positions are not uniformly spaced; they're statistically proportional to stake.

The 4-slot grouping matters because of Gulf Stream: the upcoming leader starts receiving forwarded transactions during the previous leader's last slot. This amortizes the cost of switching active leaders and is how Solana avoids the dead time you'd otherwise have between leader handoffs.

03The TPU Pipeline

"Block building" is literally one stage of this pipeline. Everything else is the leader doing leader things.

The Solana TPU pipeline. Block building lives inside Banking Stage -- everything else is plumbing.

Stage by stage

  • Fetch. Three QUIC sockets: tpu (regular), tpu_vote (validator votes), tpu_forwards (overflow from previous leader). Stake-weighted QoS reserves 80% of capacity for staked nodes. 100k packet buffer.
  • SigVerify. Ed25519 signature checks (SIMD-parallelized, GPU-capable). Dedups against the recent blockhash window. Firedancer can FPGA-accelerate this to 8M sigs/sec.
  • Banking Stage. Where transactions actually get scheduled and executed against the bank. This is where every block-building company is fighting. See section 4.
  • PoH Recorder. Single-core SHA-256 hash chain. Mixes executed transaction hashes into the chain and signs entries. Ticks every 6.25ms whether execution finished or not -- if Banking falls behind, you get a partial block.
  • Broadcast. Packs entries into ~1,200-byte shreds, applies 32:32 Reed-Solomon erasure coding (any 32 of 64 shreds can reconstruct), and pushes via Turbine's multi-hop tree.
Watch for

Talk of "submitting blocks" or "block proposals" on Solana is loose phrasing -- there is no native protocol for that today. It means submitting transaction bundles or candidate orderings that the leader chooses to honor. The leader still constructs and signs the final block.

04BankingStage: The Scheduler War

The whole game is fought here. Old Agave had a famously bad scheduler. Fixing it changed Solana economics.

⊖ Legacy (pre-v1.18)

4 worker threads, all pulling from one shared queue.

  • No thread had visibility into what others were doing
  • All 4 would race to lock the same high-priority transactions
  • A thread might lock 1 of 128 attempted txs -- 127 retries wasted
  • Lower-priority txs could land before higher-priority ones
  • Validators left ~80% of potential fees on the table

⊕ Central Scheduler (default since 2.3)

One dedicated 7th thread does scheduling. Workers wait.

  • Scheduler builds a prio-graph: edges between conflicting txs per account
  • Workers receive guaranteed conflict-free batches via private channels
  • Strict priority-fee ordering preserved across threads
  • Agave 3.1 fixed a sync-overhead bug -- banking went from 39% productive to 91%
  • Up to 80% more fees per block
Why Anza's central scheduler refactor was a Solana economics event, not just a code cleanup. Firedancer's pack tile is the same idea executed in a tiled C process model.

The priority formula change

v1.18 also fixed a perverse incentive in how priority was computed:

OLD:  priority = priority_fee / CU_requested
NEW:  priority = ((priority_fee * CU_requested) + base_fee) / (1 + CU_requested)

The old formula let you inflate your effective priority by claiming you needed huge CU without paying proportionally. The new one ties the priority score to actual compute committed.

★ Scheduler insight

Firedancer's pack tile uses fast bitsets instead of a prio-graph for conflict detection -- order-of-magnitude faster under burst load -- and follows the Goldilocks rules: don't pack too fast (you miss higher-fee txs arriving later in the slot), don't pack too slow (you leave space empty), and dynamically adjust parallelism based on remaining slot time.

05Account Locks & Hot Accounts

Why a popular AMM pool can serialize the world.

Every Solana transaction declares its account access upfront -- {pubkey, is_writable}. The scheduler reads this before executing anything and uses it to build conflict-free batches.

Access patternCan run in parallel?
Two txs both read account A✓ yes
One writes A, another reads A✗ no -- serialized
Two txs both write A✗ no -- serialized
Hot accounts (Raydium pools, Pyth feeds, popular mint authorities) hard-serialize every conflicting tx onto one worker. No priority fee can break the throughput ceiling.

This is why Solana DEX devs ship state-sharded pools, why oracle architectures cache reads to a per-consumer account, and why SIMD-0110 (exponential write-lock fees) exists. It is also why MEV bot operators care so much about which slot they hit -- once a pool is contested, ordering inside that pool is single-threaded.

Three hard ceilings that anyone building blocks must respect.

PoH is a sequential SHA-256 hash chain running on a dedicated single thread as fast as the CPU allows. It is functionally a Verifiable Delay Function -- not formally one, but sequentially preimage-resistant. Insert a tx hash into the chain at position N and the entire downstream chain depends on it; you can't retroactively reorder.

hash[N+1] = SHA256(hash[N])                          # tick
hash[N+1] = SHA256(hash[N] || SHA256(transactions))  # entry with txs

One slot = 64 ticks (6.25ms each). The PoH thread doesn't wait for execution -- if Banking falls behind, it just inserts empty ticks and the block ships partially full. There is no retry window inside the slot. Miss it, client resubmits.

Number to double-check live

Most older docs still cite the 48M CU block limit. SIMD-0256 raised it to 60M. SIMD-0286 (proposed 100M) is under discussion. The 48M figure is the common gotcha in older write-ups.

08Jito: The Incumbent

~95% of stake runs the Jito-Solana client. Understanding it is non-negotiable for following the Harmonic pitch.

What Jito-Solana actually is

A fork of Agave with three extra stages bolted into the validator:

  • RelayerStage -- intercepts TPU traffic before it hits Fetch
  • BlockEngineStage -- maintains a gRPC link to Jito's centralized auction service
  • BundleStage -- executes bundles atomically via BundleAccountLocker and writes them to PoH all-or-nothing
The Jito stack. Block engine is centralized and Jito-operated; relayer can be validator-local but most use Jito's hosted version.

Bundles

A bundle is up to 5 transactions, atomic, same-slot, strict order. It includes a transfer to one of 8 static tip PDAs. The block engine ranks competing bundles by tip-per-CU, not absolute tip. getTipAccounts returns the live PDA set.

ShredStream

Skips Turbine's multi-hop tree by proxying shreds directly from the leader to whitelisted subscribers. Saves "hundreds of milliseconds" for co-located HFT operators. Access-gated via Jito Discord whitelisting and an open UDP port -- this gate is where the "stake-weighted" privilege materializes for sophisticated searchers.

Where the tip actually goes

  • ~95-97% → the block-producing validator (split with stakers per their MEV commission)
  • 2.7% → Jito DAO treasury (via TipRouter)
  • 0.3% → TipRouter NCN operators
★ Why validators all opted in

Pre-Jito, a validator's APY to delegators was ~6-7%. Post-Jito, aggressive participation puts it at 8-10%. That delta compounds into stake migration -- over 18 months, Jito-Solana went from 48% to 95% of active stake. Block-building infrastructure became the competitive moat in validator economics. This is the structural force every later player has to displace or coexist with.

The mempool drama (March 2024)

Jito shut down its public mempool after sandwich extraction crossed $500M lifetime. The fight that ensued:

  • Jito's position: sandwiching was a pure negative externality; shutting down the public mempool was a public good.
  • Mert / Charbonneau et al.: a centralized operator unilaterally redefined the market. Sandwich activity didn't stop -- it moved to opaque private mempools (DeezNode) where validators take 50% of sandwich profits.
  • Outcome: "wide sandwich" attacks (50-300ms gaps, exploiting the relayer delay rather than tight bundle ordering) increased ~30*.

This is the political backdrop to Harmonic's "open block building" framing. If you can't trust a single operator to censor toxic MEV correctly, the answer (Harmonic argues) is to let validators choose between competing builders with different policies.

10Harmonic: The Multi-Builder Marketplace

Paradigm-backed, $6M seed, NYC ~10 people, spun out of Temporal (the team behind Nozomi and HumidiFi). Live since November 2025.

The thesis in one paragraph

Validators today have one practical block-building partner: Jito. That's a single point of failure, a single auction operator with full visibility into every searcher's strategy, a single MEV philosophy imposed on every block. Harmonic's pitch: let many builders compete; let the validator pick.

Harmonic's architecture. The engine is centralized (today operated by Temporal), but the builders feeding it are independent. Validators retain final selection.

What the validator actually installs

A component called harmonic/proposer, marketed as a "drop-in client" that runs alongside an existing Agave validator. Whether it's a sidecar process or a forked client binary is not fully spelled out in public docs -- an open question. Onboarding requires manual whitelisting + a "builder binding" applied by the Harmonic team.

Jito launched their IBRL Explorer in January 2026 specifically to highlight what they called Harmonic's "late packing" -- transactions clustered at the end of slots, evidence of delayed state propagation. Harmonic's counter: blocks look late-packed because the 50ms microblock auction resolves at final ticks; the right metric is throughput per slot, not packing distribution. Expect this to come up.

⚠ The vertical-integration question

Harmonic is operated by Temporal, which also runs Nozomi (a tx-landing service) and HumidiFi (a market-making DEX that processed ~60% of Solana spot volume at peak). Independent research (Blockworks) found HumidiFi's oracle-update win rate was 73% on Harmonic validators vs 37-52% elsewhere in the period before late January 2026. Harmonic says that advantage has since disappeared. This is the sharpest open critique of the project.

12The Protocol Roadmap

What Alpenglow, APE, and Constellation do to the block-building market. The forward-looking part of the lecture lives here.

The roadmap that turns every "what about X?" question in the Q&A into a forward-looking discussion.

Alpenglow Q3 2026

Replaces TowerBFT with Votor (two-path BLS-aggregated voting: ≥80% stake → 100ms finality, ≥60% → 150ms) and Turbine with Rotor (single-hop shred propagation, ships separately). Votes go off-chain, freeing ~75% of ledger bandwidth. Leader rotation itself does not change.

For block builders: the finality window shrinks from 12.8s to ~150ms. Dark MEV (timing manipulation within a leader slot) becomes much more expensive because early-slot delays are penalized harder than late ones.

APE -- Async Program Execution 2027+

Separates consensus from execution. The block becomes a pure ordering commitment -- leaders seal ordering without knowing execution outcomes. Two domains:

  • Vote Execution Domain (VED) -- fast lane: VoteProgram, SystemProgram, fee payers. Leader executes immediately.
  • User Execution Domain (UED) -- slow lane: all DeFi, NFTs, games. Executed by validators on the heaviest fork only.

Crucially, APE introduces formal builders via UserBlockSlots -- staked nodes separate from leaders who construct non-vote UserBlocks. Default 2 per regular slot. This is the layer Harmonic is built for. If APE ships, Harmonic's "aggregate competing builders" model goes from useful overlay to native protocol fit.

Constellation (MCP / MCL) Late 2026 -- 2027

Adds two new roles on top of Alpenglow: ~16 Proposers on a 50ms cycle and 256 Attesters. Any pslice attested by ≥40% of attesters must be included by the leader, or the block is cryptographically invalid. Hard censorship resistance enforced at the protocol layer.

Fee restructure: ordering (priority) fees go to all validators by stake weight, not just the leader. This compresses the exclusive MEV advantage current Jito-optimized validators hold.

Firedancer Live, expanding

Frankendancer (Firedancer networking + Agave runtime) at ~26% stake, zero consensus divergence over 50k+ blocks. Full Firedancer producing mainnet blocks since Oct 2025, ~5% stake. Provides true client diversity and the deterministic sub-millisecond block construction latency that Rotor needs. The pack tile already implements Goldilocks-style block packing.

13Future of Off-Validator Block Building

If APE + Constellation + ACE all ship, what survives for Jito and Harmonic?

⊕ Bull case (Harmonic survives + grows)

  • APE creates a formal builder market -- Harmonic IS the aggregation layer for that market
  • Constellation removes proximity advantage → builders compete on quality, not just latency
  • Searchers still need order-flow routing; protocol-level inclusion doesn't pick the best builder
  • Builder specialization (perps, arb, NFT mints) keeps the marketplace useful
  • TEE / ACE proxies (BAM-style) fill the gap until protocol-level ACE ships in 2027+

⊖ Bear case (absorbed into the protocol)

  • If Constellation + APE + ACE all land, the distinct "block building" layer collapses into protocol roles
  • Stake-weighted inclusion guarantees remove the need for tip-based censorship workarounds
  • App-owned sequencing (ACE) moves ordering control to the smart contract, not the builder
  • Harmonic narrows to a thin builder-aggregation/preference-tooling layer
  • Jito's moat shifts to JitoSOL + BAM TEE; Harmonic's moat must shift to something not on the roadmap

14Open Questions for Harmonic

Ordered from warm-up to sharpest.

Q1 · Warm-up · architecture

The harmonic/proposer component -- is it a sidecar process alongside Agave, a patched fork, or a library? What's the end-to-end gRPC latency budget from builder submission to validator block selection?

Q2 · Engineering · scheduler

Agave's central scheduler uses a prio-graph; Firedancer's pack tile uses fast bitsets with Goldilocks packing rules. How does Harmonic's engine reason about cross-builder block scoring when each builder is using a different internal scheduling strategy?

Q3 · Economics · fee redistribution

Constellation moves ordering fees from "leader takes all" to "all validators by stake weight." Does that expand your market (every validator now cares about block quality) or compress it (leaders no longer pocket the upside that funded Jito's flywheel)?

Q4 · Forward-looking · APE

If APE ships and formalizes UserBlockSlots as a protocol-level builder role, does Harmonic become a builder, a builder aggregator, or something else? How does your current architecture map to APE's builder model?

Q5 · Latency / packing

Jito's IBRL Explorer surfaced "late packing" on Harmonic validators. Your counter is that the 50ms microblock auction resolves at final ticks. What metric do you publish that you'd want the validator community to evaluate builder quality on instead?

Q6 · Sharpest · vertical integration

Temporal operates Harmonic, Nozomi, and HumidiFi. Blockworks reported HumidiFi's oracle-update win rate was 73% on Harmonic validators pre-late-January, vs 37-52% elsewhere. What changed technically when that gap closed, and what's the structural firewall between Harmonic's auction logic and HumidiFi's order flow going forward?

Q7 · Future-proofing · ACE

Protocol-level ACE is 2027+. BAM covers the gap with TEE-secured execution but is single-operator. Does Harmonic plan a TEE / verifiability layer for external builders, or are you betting validators will accept the open marketplace's reputation model instead?

16One Closing Frame

If you carry one frame away from all of this, make it this:

Solana's block-building market is a temporary shape. It exists because the protocol has a monolithic leader and no PBS. Every company in this space is a bet on what fills the gap between today's leader-does-everything model and tomorrow's protocol-level builder roles. Jito's bet is "be the integrated incumbent." Harmonic's bet is "be the open marketplace." Anza's bet is "fix it at the protocol layer and these companies become thin overlays." The next 18 months decide which bet pays.
